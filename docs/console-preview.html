Attack surface management where the AI does triage, not discovery.

kiyooo doesn't reimplement subdomain scanners or vulnerability crawlers — it wraps best-in-class open-source tools (Prowler, Trivy, TruffleHog, apktool, plus its own passive recon) and puts an LLM adjudicator in front of every hit they produce. A raw finding is a candidate, never something shown to a human on its own — it has to survive category matching and cited-evidence adjudication first. Below is the complete console: the original recon/triage/cost pipeline (Stages 0–12) plus the cloud, containers, repo, and mobile modules added on top of it (Stages 13–17) — one nav, one pipeline, nothing set aside.

The pipeline

Every finding takes the same road, regardless of source

Whether a hit comes from Prowler scanning an AWS account or from kiyooo's own recon against your external perimeter, it goes through the identical five steps before a person ever sees it.

01

Discover

Prowler / Trivy / TruffleHog / apktool run against your own infra — never a third party's.

02

Ingest

Vendor severity is discarded. raw_severity always comes from the matched category.

03

Adjudicate

An LLM verdict, every claim cited to an evidence ID. It has no authority to close anything.

04

Review

A human confirms true/false-positive. Nothing auto-closes a critical.

05

Route

A ticket drafts. A human sends it — sinks never fire on their own.

This is the whole differentiator: discovery is a commodity, adjudication isn't. A false-positive rate a human has to wade through is the actual product failure kiyooo exists to prevent.

Proving it against something real

Three demo labs, one command

Self-contained, offline, resettable Docker environments that plant real instances of kiyooo's shipped categories — including their false-positive paths — so a demo isn't just a screenshot. kiyoo-labs starts all three and adds every /etc/hosts entry they need.

acmecorp

Conventional attack surface: exposed DB, leaked secrets, an expired cert, an SSO false-positive, a WAF-reduced severity, a seeded-data downgrade. Everything binds to 127.0.0.1 only.

$ kiyoo-labs acmecorp
Stage 12. Fake hosts like staging.acmecorp.lab, admin.acmecorp.lab.

kiyoo-ai

The AI attack surface above, planted for real: MCP twin servers (harmless / dangerous / poisoned), a vector store, a model registry, a notebook server, a shadow AI SaaS tenant, plus a drift scenario.

$ kiyoo-labs ai
Stage 11b's lab. ~20 services behind one Host-routed FastAPI app.

kiyoo-range

Not a planted-asset lab — the interactive front door onto both of the above. One page per shipped category (13 infra + 10 AI), each with a live-evidence fetch against the real decoy, not a static link.

$ kiyoo-labs range
Also serves 4 categories with no other planted home: exposed-devops-console, subdomain-takeover, known-exploited-cve, shadow-saas-tenant.

Why it's safe to run against yourself

Eight invariants the code never gets to break

Not aspirations — enforced at the network-executor and pipeline layer, regardless of what a task or a prompt asks for.

01

No exploitation primitives. Verification proves reachability, never compromise.

02

All outbound traffic routes through ScopeGuard, enforced at the executor, never the planner.

03

Passive by default. Active scanning needs a config flag, a CLI flag, and an attestation.

04

The LLM has no authority. It cites evidence; it never closes a finding.

05

Evidence is data, never instruction — even an instruction found inside it is just a finding to report.

06

A human approves outbound. Sinks draft; people send.

07

Secrets are redacted at ingest — a credential_ref pointer, never a live value.

08

Models are pinned. Changing one needs an eval re-run and a changelog entry.
